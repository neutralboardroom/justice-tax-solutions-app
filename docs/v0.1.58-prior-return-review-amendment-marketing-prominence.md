# Justice Tax Solutions v0.1.58 - Prior-return review / amendment opportunity marketing prominence

## Purpose

v0.1.58 makes Prior Return Review / Amendment Opportunity Check a highly prominent public feature across the homepage, navigation language, start flow, dashboard, pricing/help, FAQ, staff guidance, and marketing materials.

The feature is now positioned as a main Justice Tax Solutions offer alongside filing a new personal return, filing a new business return, uploading a tax notice for the Free Tax Problem Truth Check, and fixing a tax problem.

## Public positioning

Core safe message:

> Already filed? We can review your prior tax returns to see whether changes may be available that could help. If an amended return may be appropriate, we can explain your options and help prepare and file it if you choose paid help.

Prominent homepage language includes:

- Already Filed? Your Past Returns May Be Worth a Second Look.
- Review Past Returns for Possible Missed Savings.
- Filed already? A second look may uncover something worth correcting.
- Upload recent tax returns and see whether an amended return may be worth considering.

## Preserved safety posture

This build does not activate:

- final IRS/NYS/NYC official form output
- live sensitive taxpayer uploads
- e-file
- agency submission
- Refund Transfer
- refund advance
- direct debit
- guaranteed refunds/savings
- guaranteed amended-return acceptance
- CPA/EA/attorney/law-firm/accounting-firm claims

## Required careful language

Use:

- may
- possible
- worth reviewing
- may be appropriate
- could help
- no guarantee
- professional review may be needed

Do not use:

- We will get you more money back.
- We guarantee a bigger refund.
- We guarantee savings.
- We guarantee your amended return will be accepted.
- We guarantee the IRS or state will approve the change.
- We amend returns for free.
- We can recover money for everyone.

## Deadline language

Refund and amendment deadlines can matter. For federal refund claims, IRS Form 1040-X instructions generally say a claim must be filed within 3 years after the original return was filed or 2 years after the tax was paid, whichever is later, but deadlines, exceptions, and state/city rules should be verified before action.
